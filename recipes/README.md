## The shape they all share

CI already has the three things a deck needs: **data**, **a trigger**, and **artifact plumbing**. The
action supplies the last mile.

```yaml
- run: python scripts/build_deck.py > deck.json     # your data → slide intents
- uses: smartdatabrokers/slideforge-deck-action@v1  # intents → .pptx
  with:
    api-key: ${{ secrets.SLIDEFORGE_API_KEY }}
```

**`deck.json` is normally generated by the step before it**, not hand-written. The action is
deliberately dumb; the interesting part is the script that maps your data onto slide forms. Write that
once, and the deck rebuilds itself forever.

## Why this belongs in CI specifically

CI hates nondeterminism, and rendering here is deterministic: **same input → same deck**, and your
numbers are bound **verbatim** — there is no model in the render path inventing or "improving" a figure.
That's what makes it safe to put a generated deck in front of a board or a customer.

Every run also reports what happened (`status`, `fidelity`, warnings), and the step's exit code follows
it — so a deck that didn't render faithfully **fails the build instead of shipping quietly**. And a
render that isn't usable [never bills](https://slideforge.dev/pricing).

## Picking forms

Each slide is a `form` plus its typed fields. Browse all 150+ layouts and their exact payload schemas at
[slideforge.dev/templates](https://slideforge.dev/templates). The recipes here use:

`hero_statement` (covers) · `kpi_metrics` (metric boards) · `trend_chart` (series over time) ·
`bar_rank_chart` (ranked magnitudes) · `status_dashboard` (RAG workstream tables) · `agenda_list`
(grouped lists)
